Read against the right benchmark, the long window is the bad news

PSI bands are defined for 24-hour exposure. Scoring an annual mean against them is a category error — a year-long average belongs against an annual guideline. Against WHO's, Singapore's actual annual mean does not look reassuring at all.

Method

The conversion

NEA's PM2.5 sub-index is a segmented linear function with breakpoints at (0,0) (12,50) (55,100) (150,200) (250,300) (350,400) (500,500), interpolated by their Equation 1. Verified against NEA's own worked example: 40 µg/m³ → 83.

Two averaging orders

PSI is concave — 4.17 index points per µg/m³ on the first segment, 0.67 on the last. By Jensen's inequality averaging the published index lands at or below averaging the concentrations, shaving another 23 points off the 24-hour figure. Both orders are computed.

Why the number falls

A rolling mean is a low-pass filter, and haze arrives as episodes lasting days against a clean baseline — precisely the signal a long window removes. The figure drops because the averaging discarded the event, not because the air cleared.

What it cannot do

Reach “safe”. The Good band needs a mean at or under 12 µg/m³; the real annual mean is —. No window length available produces that claim — the ladder bottoms out at Moderate.
